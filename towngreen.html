TOWN GREEN

Designing a tool to inform the community

OVERVIEW

The Town Green project represents a US $180 million mixed-use development in Scotts Valley, California — including housing, retail, restaurants, public art, and green spaces. As the development team prepared for the community’s first event, they needed a digital platform to inform residents, collect interest, and build an early subscriber base. I was tasked with building that platform from scratch.

GOAL

Launch a clean, responsive, mobile-first website in two weeks, in time for the first community event — and collect contact information from visitors to allow the development team to communicate updates as the project evolves.

ROLE

I served as the sole designer and developer. Responsibilities included:

  • Designing the site from the ground up (mobile-first, then scaled to desktop)
  • Building and implementing the subscription and contact system
  • Managing and updating the subscriber database and community sign-ups

CHALLENGE & CONTEXT

  • No existing website or assets: At project start, there was no prior web presence, no visual identity, and little content.
  • Lack of evocative imagery: The project didn’t yet exist physically — using the empty land’s photos felt uninspiring. The challenge was to convey the potential and aspiration of the project.
  • Time pressure: The site needed to be ready within two weeks for the first community event — a tight deadline that required fast decisions and prioritization.

The Solution

VISUAL STORYTELLING & BRAND MOOD

To evoke the feel of a welcoming community space, I chose to use evocative photos of plazas, green spaces, and community areas in California — rather than empty land. This helped communicate the vision of Town Green as a gathering place, rather than just a real estate development.

MOBILE-FIRST DESIGN + SCALABLE LAYOUT

Starting with a mobile layout allowed for quicker prototyping and ensured accessibility for many users. Once the mobile foundation was solid, the design was easily expanded to a desktop version, making the site future-proof and responsive on all devices.

CLEAR INFORMATION ARCHITECTURE & PRIORITY-DRIVEN SECTIONS

The site was structured with user and community needs in mind:

  • Hero section with a concise message + call-to-action (CTA) to subscribe.
  • Events section to list planned and past community events.
  • Opportunities / Involvement section — for users who couldn’t attend events but still wanted to contribute or stay informed.
  • Subscription form with minimal required fields (to lower friction and maximize sign-ups).
  • This structure ensured that the site delivered the most important value — connecting the community and the development team — in a straightforward way.

Results & Impact

From August 2018 to February 2019 (6 months after launch),
the website achieved:
  • 125 survey responses
  • 181 email subscribers
  • 1,915 unique visitors
  • 3,617 online page views

These numbers demonstrate early community interest, engagement, and success at building a subscriber base — exactly what the site aimed to achieve.

Reflection & Lessons Learned

  • Speed + clarity wins: With limited time and no preexisting brand assets, prioritizing a clear layout, essential content, and a strong CTA made it possible to launch rapidly and still deliver value.
  • Imagery builds connection: Using evocative community-space photos helped communicate a vision and emotional tone — offering prospective residents a glimpse of what could be, even before anything was built.
  • Keep friction low: Minimizing required fields in the subscription form significantly helped lower barriers to sign-up — an important lesson for any community-oriented or lead-generation site.
  • Start simple, iterate later: Launching a barebones but functional site on time created a foundation. Future iterations (with more content, features, or branding) can build on that foundation without rewriting everything.
