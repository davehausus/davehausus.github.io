Designing for caregiving begins with understanding the people experiencing it.

Care is shaped by relationships, responsibilities, personal circumstances, routines, expectations, and changing needs. The experience is rarely the same for everyone involved. Caregivers and care recipients may share the same goal while experiencing different concerns, pressures, and perspectives.

This section builds a grounded understanding of those realities. It brings together research evidence, behavioral insights, human needs, and lived contexts to help us move beyond assumptions and make more thoughtful product decisions.

## Understanding people beyond their roles

Caregiving is not defined by a single profile or situation. People navigate different levels of responsibility, emotional involvement, confidence, independence, and familiarity with technology. These factors influence how they perceive information, make decisions, build trust, and interact with Nomo.

Our understanding must consider both individual experiences and the relationships between people. The needs of a caregiver cannot automatically represent those of the care recipient, and supporting one person should not come at the expense of the other's autonomy, dignity, or sense of control.

## From evidence to experience decisions

Understanding people is valuable only when it informs what we design and why.

The insights developed throughout this section help us recognize meaningful needs, identify friction, understand behavior, anticipate challenges, and evaluate design decisions against real circumstances.

They inform how we:

- Structure information and interactions around people's mental models.
- Design for different motivations, capabilities, routines, and contexts.
- Support independence without disregarding the need for reassurance.
- Communicate clearly during routine, uncertain, or stressful situations.
- Recognize barriers and provide appropriate guidance, feedback, and recovery.
- Identify assumptions that require further research or validation.

## An evolving understanding

Our understanding of people is never complete. Research, feedback, observed behavior, and changing circumstances may challenge what we previously believed.

We must distinguish evidence from interpretation and assumptions, remain attentive to gaps in our knowledge, and revisit our decisions as new insights emerge.

The goal is not to create definitive descriptions of people. It is to build a more informed, empathetic, and continually evolving understanding that helps Nomo deliver experiences grounded in human reality.
