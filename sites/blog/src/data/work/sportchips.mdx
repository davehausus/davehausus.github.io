Sportchips provides performance tuning configurations for vehicles. Its website needed a complete redesign after years without a major update. The existing experience had been built for desktop, making navigation and product discovery difficult on newer devices.

The main challenge was helping customers find the tuning options available for their specific vehicle while making detailed technical information easier to navigate.

## The challenge

### Replacing an outdated website

The original website dated back to 2008 and had no responsive layout. Its structure needed to adapt to mobile, tablet, and desktop screens without making the existing product information unfamiliar to returning customers.

I approached the redesign as a broader restructuring of the website, covering its layout, navigation, content hierarchy, and product presentation.

### Connecting vehicles to compatible products

Tuning configurations depend on the vehicle being serviced. Customers needed a reliable way to identify the options available for their make, model, and year rather than searching through a general catalog or contacting support to clarify compatibility.

This became a central requirement for the new experience: vehicle selection had to lead directly to relevant product information.

### Making technical specifications easier to scan

Performance tuning products require detailed specifications and performance information. Presenting those details clearly meant establishing a hierarchy that helped customers identify relevant information without having to interpret an undifferentiated block of technical content.

## Design approach

### A responsive structure built around key tasks

I redesigned the site's page layouts and information architecture for mobile, tablet, and desktop. The new structure gave greater prominence to two important actions: finding compatible tuning products and requesting a quote.

Typography, spacing, and a more consistent visual hierarchy helped separate sections and make information easier to scan. The aim was to improve access to existing content while bringing the website up to date across screen sizes.

### Building the Vehicle Finder

The Vehicle Finder was a custom module developed with a dedicated development team. I coordinated its development as part of the broader website redesign.

Users could select their vehicle's make, model, and year to find the tuning configurations available for it. The results presented relevant products with images, key features, and options to request a quote.

This connected vehicle selection to the product catalog in a more direct way, reducing the need for customers to determine compatibility by navigating unrelated product pages.

### Organizing product information

I redesigned the product pages to give images, specifications, and performance details a clearer order. Rather than presenting every detail with equal emphasis, the layouts made the main product information easier to locate and review.

This was particularly important for a catalog where customers needed to understand the differences between configurations before deciding which option to enquire about.

### Updating the visual system

The redesign introduced a cleaner visual language through more consistent layouts, restrained color accents, and a coordinated icon style. These changes supported the new information hierarchy and gave the site's different sections a more coherent appearance.

The visual refresh was part of the larger restructuring, rather than a standalone change to the site's styling.

## My role

I led the website redesign and development, covering UI/UX design, layout, information architecture, and database management. I also coordinated with a development team on the Vehicle Finder module.

My responsibilities spanned the overall website experience and the structure connecting vehicle compatibility, product information, and quote requests.

## Outcome

The redesigned website introduced a responsive experience across devices and a dedicated path for finding tuning configurations by vehicle. Customers could move from selecting their vehicle to reviewing compatible products and requesting a quote.

Product pages also presented technical information in a more structured format, making the catalog easier to navigate.

The available project information does not include analytics or post-launch research, so the effect on conversion rates, support requests, or customer behavior cannot be quantified.

## Reflection

This project highlighted how product discovery depends on the relationship between information architecture and the underlying data. A responsive layout improved access across devices, but the Vehicle Finder addressed a more specific problem: connecting a customer's vehicle to the products that actually applied to it.

Designing the interface meant accounting for that relationship throughout the experience, from vehicle selection to product details and quote requests.
