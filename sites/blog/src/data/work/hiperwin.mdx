Hiperwin was developing three product lines with different purposes and audiences: Hiperware for home appliances, Hiperclean for cleaning products, and Hipertech for technology accessories. Each needed a recognizable identity, but the products also had to feel like part of the same company.

I worked as the sole designer across the project, from the master brand and product line logos to packaging, printed materials, and the ecommerce interface. My task was to establish a shared visual language and apply it consistently across these different formats.

## Designing the brand system

I started with the Hiperwin master logo. Its concept used geometric shapes to represent the company's product line families. Those shapes became the foundation of the identity, giving the main brand a visual connection to the individual product categories.

### Product line logos

Each product line received its own logo built around a distinct geometric shape. The goal was to give each category an identifiable visual character without creating three unrelated identities.

- **Hiperware:** A shape intended to convey stability and utility.
- **Hiperclean:** A shape intended to communicate lightness and clarity.
- **Hipertech:** A shape intended to suggest precision and modernity.

The master logo brought these elements together, while each sub-brand used its own symbol. This relationship established a consistent structure for the identity and gave me a common set of elements to carry into packaging and digital applications.

### Color and typography

I developed a color palette with distinct accents for the three product lines, keeping the colors within a shared tonal range to maintain their relationship to the master brand.

Typography supported the same system. The selections prioritized legibility across different applications, from prominent product and category labels to smaller text on printed materials.

## Packaging and print materials

With the identity established, I designed packaging for multiple products across Hiperware, Hiperclean, and Hipertech, along with supporting print materials.

The packaging used a modular approach based on the brand's geometric shapes, logos, and color assignments. This gave the layouts a shared structure while allowing each product line to retain its own visual identity.

The main considerations were:

- Maintaining a consistent layout system across different products.
- Giving each product line a clear visual identifier.
- Establishing a readable hierarchy for product information.
- Adapting the identity to the requirements of individual packaging formats.

The geometric symbols served as recurring visual anchors. Rather than treating every package as an isolated design, I could use the same identity elements across the product range while adjusting each layout to its specific contents.

## Extending the identity into ecommerce

The online store was the digital application of the same system. I designed the ecommerce interface to carry the identity beyond the packaging while accommodating the company's product catalog.

### Visual continuity

The interface reused the product line colors, geometric symbols, and typography established during the branding phase. The relationship between the physical products and the online store came from these shared elements rather than from creating a separate visual language for digital.

This consistency also provided a visual basis for distinguishing the three product categories throughout the shopping experience.

### Interface structure

I designed the ecommerce layout around three practical requirements:

- Clear navigation between the product lines.
- Readable product descriptions and specifications.
- Consistent spacing and visual hierarchy across the interface.

The packaging and ecommerce work required the identity to operate at different scales and in different contexts. On packaging, the system organized product information within a physical layout. Online, the same visual elements helped structure the catalog and identify each product line.

## Project outcome

The project brought together several parts of the Hiperwin brand under one visual system:

- A master brand identity for Hiperwin.
- Three product line logos for Hiperware, Hiperclean, and Hipertech.
- Packaging for multiple products across the three categories.
- Supporting print materials.
- An ecommerce interface applying the identity to the online catalog.

As the sole designer, I was responsible for connecting the initial identity concept to its applications across print and web. The central design decision was to build the product line identities around a shared set of geometric elements, giving each category its own visual character while keeping it connected to Hiperwin.
