Athlytix was an iPad app released in 2014 for players, parents, and coaches who wanted to track basketball statistics during live games. Users could create teams and record rebounds, turnovers, assists, made and missed shots, and shot locations on the court.

I led the product design and brand identity. The interface centered on a full basketball court, with player selection and game actions arranged around it so users could record events directly on the playing area.

## Finding a balance between simplicity and detail

The competing products I examined took different approaches to basketball stat tracking.

Hoopmetrics presented a limited set of statistics around a vertically oriented half-court diagram. Its flat interface used white backgrounds for the controls, making interactive elements difficult to distinguish from other screen elements. Home and away teams had separate colors, but the main stat-entry screen had no visible branding. The interface offered relatively little visual hierarchy beyond a basic sans-serif typeface.

TurboStats exposed a much broader range of statistics, making it feel closer to a professional scoring tool. However, its gray, beveled controls resembled Windows 95-era desktop software. Most buttons looked alike, and some were small, making individual actions harder to find. It also used a vertical half court, with a small logo at the top of the screen.

Despite their differences, both products gave the basketball court a relatively narrow role in the interface. Their stat-entry screens also offered limited visual differentiation between controls and little branding.

I wanted Athlytix to sit between these approaches. It would offer enough statistics for everyday players, parents, and coaches without trying to accommodate every metric a professional tool might need. The interface also needed a clearer hierarchy, larger controls, and a visual language connected to basketball.

## Building the interface around the court

### Using the full iPad screen

I designed a full basketball court in a horizontal orientation rather than placing a half court vertically in the middle of the screen.

This gave the court more room across the iPad display and allowed the interface to accommodate player controls and game actions without crowding the playing area. Since Athlytix did not aim to expose every possible statistic, the layout could prioritize the controls relevant to its intended use.

The court became the main organizing element of the screen, connecting player selection, action entry, and shot location in one visual space.

### Recording an action directly on the court

The stat-entry interaction followed three steps:

1. Select an active player.
2. Choose an action from the controls aligned along the bottom of the screen.
3. Touch the appropriate location on the court to record the action.

I made the action controls larger than those in the competing interfaces, giving them more room and making them easier to distinguish. Keeping them together along the bottom of the screen also established a consistent place to look when selecting an action.

The final step connected the selected action to a location on the court. Rather than treating shot tracking as a separate form, the interface let users record the event directly on the playing area.

### Communicating motion to developers

Static screens couldn't fully explain how an interaction should unfold, so I used After Effects to animate interface behaviors and shared the clips with developers as visual specifications. I provided the animations as GIFs or MP4 files.

These references demonstrated details such as the scale transition when a new action appeared on the court. Developers used them to interpret the intended behavior and timing during implementation. The animations provided a concrete reference for motion that would have been difficult to communicate through static screens alone.

### Showing recorded actions and correcting mistakes

Recorded actions appeared as dots on the court. When a new action was added, its dot started larger and then shrank to its normal size. This brief scale transition helped distinguish the latest entry from the dots already on the court.

I also designed a long-press interaction for undoing actions. Users could press and hold a dot to remove the corresponding entry, correcting a mistake directly at its location instead of searching elsewhere in the interface.

### Distinguishing teams and actions

I used separate color conventions for teams and game actions. Orange and blue distinguished the two teams, while green and red differentiated positive and negative actions.

Assists, rebounds, blocks, and steals used green. Fouls and turnovers used red. This gave the action controls a clearer visual distinction than the largely uniform buttons in the competing products.

The court used a natural hardwood color, while the surrounding interface used a darker hardwood treatment. This contrast separated the playing area from the controls and kept the court as the main visual reference point.

## Developing the brand from the product

I began by designing screens for the app. Once I had a clearer understanding of the product and its interaction model, I developed the brand identity, including the symbol, wordmark, app icon, and color palette.

### A symbol that combines the name and the sport

The symbol combines a lowercase "a" with a basketball hoop. Inside the letter's counter, lines reference the seams of a basketball. A small circle beside the letter represents the location where a shot was made.

The mark connects the product name with the sport and one of the app's core functions.

### A wordmark inspired by sports typography

I used a condensed typeface with angular letterforms that resemble numbers. The wordmark is set in uppercase, with the "Y" represented by a stylized number 4 whose upper angle remains open.

Orange became the primary brand color, referencing the color of a basketball. The identity drew on recognizable elements of the sport rather than generic technology imagery.

### Bringing the identity into the interface

I incorporated the brand directly into the court. The symbol sat at the center, with wordmarks along both sides, following the placement of branding on a basketball court.

The court served as both the main spatial interface for recording game events and a surface for expressing the Athlytix identity. This connected the brand to the product's core interaction rather than confining it to an app icon or a small logo above the controls.

## A shipped product from 2014

Athlytix shipped for the iPad in 2014 and is no longer available in the App Store. The original design files preserve the interface, interaction details, and brand explorations.

The design centered on a specific trade-off: providing useful basketball statistics without overwhelming the person recording them. The full-court layout, bottom-aligned action controls, location-based input, and direct undo interaction were the main ways I addressed that challenge.
